# Digital Property Rights Act

**Restoring property rights in consumer digital goods.**

This repository houses a narrow, passable legislative proposal: if a company advertises a consumer digital good using words like **buy**, **purchase**, **own**, or **sale**, the buyer should receive ownership of one usable copy.

## Core principle

> Property rights should not disappear simply because property becomes digital.

The proposal is intentionally narrow. It does **not** require perpetual hosting, free upgrades, ongoing support, multiplayer servers, cloud services, or mandatory digital resale in the first bill. It preserves copyright law and does not legalize piracy. It simply makes purchase language legally meaningful.

## Signature rule

If a consumer digital good is advertised as a purchase, the seller must deliver one usable copy that the purchaser can download, retain, back up, and use without ongoing seller authentication, except where the product is clearly sold as a rental, subscription, or service.

## What this is not

This is not anti-subscription, anti-streaming, anti-cloud, or anti-copyright. A service can still be sold as a service. A rental can still be sold as a rental. But if the seller says **buy**, the consumer should get a copy.

## Proposed first legislative lane

Start with a federal truth-in-digital-sale and delivery-right bill. Do not lead with digital resale. Resale is important, but it triggers a harder copyright and technical debate. The first passable bill should focus on honest purchase language and the right to receive and retain a usable copy.
