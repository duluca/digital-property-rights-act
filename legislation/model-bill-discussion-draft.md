# Discussion Draft

Digital Property Rights Act


To require that consumer digital goods advertised as purchases transfer ownership of a usable copy, and for other purposes.

## Section 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “Digital Property Rights Act.”

## Section 2. Findings

Consumers reasonably understand “buy,” “purchase,” “own,” and “sale” to mean that ownership of a copy transfers.

Digital commerce has increasingly used purchase language for transactions that provide only revocable access.

Ownership of a copy is distinct from ownership of copyright.

Federal law should preserve copyright protections while preventing misleading consumer-facing sales language.

## Section 3. Definitions

(1) Consumer digital good means a fixed digital file or package of files offered as a discrete product to consumers, including a digital audiovisual work, audio work, ebook, application, downloadable game, software installer, font, digital artwork, 3D model, or course file.

(2) Digital service means an ongoing remote service whose essential value depends on continuing server-side operation, including streaming catalogs, cloud gaming, massively multiplayer online games, software-as-a-service, APIs, generative AI services, multiplayer servers, live databases, and social platforms.

(3) Sale means a transaction that transfers ownership of one usable copy of a consumer digital good.

## Section 4. Prohibited misleading digital sales language

It shall be unlawful for any person engaged in interstate commerce to advertise, offer, or complete a consumer transaction using the terms “buy,” “purchase,” “own,” “sale,” or substantially similar language for a consumer digital good unless the transaction transfers ownership of one usable copy of that consumer digital good to the purchaser.

## Section 5. Minimum incidents of ownership

The right to download or otherwise receive the copy within a reasonable period disclosed before payment.

The right to retain the copy indefinitely.

The right to store the copy locally or in personal cloud storage controlled by the purchaser.

The right to make personal backup copies for the purchaser’s lawful personal use.

The right to migrate the copy to new storage media or compatible formats for lawful personal use.

The right to use the copy without ongoing dependence on the seller’s authentication servers, except for features clearly disclosed and sold as digital services.

## Section 6. No perpetual hosting or upgrade obligation

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to require a seller to host, store, redeliver, patch, update, upgrade, remaster, maintain, or support a consumer digital good after delivery unless the seller separately and expressly undertakes such obligation.

## Section 7. Service, rental, and subscription disclosures

A transaction that does not transfer ownership of a consumer digital good shall be clearly and conspicuously labeled before payment as access, rental, subscription, license, or digital service, as applicable, and shall disclose whether access may expire, be revoked, or depend on continuing operation of remote servers.

## Section 8. Copyright preservation

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to transfer ownership of copyright or authorize public distribution, public performance, public display, circumvention for infringement, or reproduction beyond personal ownership uses otherwise permitted by law.

## Section 9. Safe harbor

A seller shall be deemed to satisfy Sections 4 and 5 by providing a DRM-free or offline-usable copy of the consumer digital good within a reasonable download window disclosed before payment, together with plain-language disclosures of any features requiring continuing remote services.

## Section 10. Enforcement

A violation of this Act shall be treated as an unfair or deceptive act or practice. Enforcement may be by the Federal Trade Commission and state attorneys general. The Act does not create a private right of action in this discussion draft, but Congress may add one if desired.

## Section 11. Study on digital transferability

The Register of Copyrights and the Federal Trade Commission shall jointly study whether and how owners of consumer digital goods may transfer ownership of a copy without retaining additional usable copies, including platform transfer, third-party registry, cryptographic certification, and attestation models, and shall report recommendations to Congress within 24 months.
